Why Syno Manager

A native app, not a web console

Checking whether a backup ran should not mean signing in to DSM and driving a browser UI built for a mouse. Syno Manager reads the same APIs DSM's own web interface uses and lays the answers out natively - on a phone, a tablet, a Mac or a PC, each one adapting to the screen it is on.

⚡

Live, not cached

CPU, memory, temperature, disk I/O and per-interface throughput update while you watch, at an interval you choose.

🧩

Shaped by your NAS

The app asks your NAS what it can do and hides everything it cannot. No dead buttons for packages you do not run.

🔒

Direct connection

Your device talks to your NAS. Nothing passes through a MiyaraHub server, because there isn't one.

Privacy

There is no server in the middle

Syno Manager is a client for your NAS, not a service. Your phone opens a connection to your DiskStation and that is the whole architecture.

  • No MiyaraHub server. Nothing about your NAS reaches us, because there is nowhere in the path for it to go.
  • Credentials in the keychain. Held in the platform's secure storage, never in plain text, and excluded from device backups.
  • 2FA and device trust. One-time codes are supported, and a trusted device is remembered so you are not asked every launch.
  • The assistant is on-device. Your question, your NAS data and the answer never leave the phone.
  • One thing does leave: crash reports, so a crash can be fixed. They carry a stack trace, not your NAS. The whole picture.

Common questions

Synology on a phone, answered

What does Syno Manager do?
It puts the parts of DSM you actually check on a phone into one app: live CPU, RAM, temperature and network readings, storage volumes and per-drive SMART health, Docker containers, Package Center, Surveillance Station live view, Synology Photos and Drive, Hyper Backup and Active Backup, Download Station, File Station, users, scheduled tasks, UPS, DDNS and Wake-on-LAN. It talks to your NAS directly using DSM's own documented APIs. The User Guide covers every screen.
Which Synology models does it work with?
Any Synology NAS running DSM 7.0 or later. The app reads what your particular NAS reports on connect and only shows the screens it actually supports, so a two-bay DS223j and a rackmount RS1221+ each get a dashboard that matches what they can do. See compatibility.
Does it work when I am away from home?
Yes. Add your NAS once and the app uses whatever route is reachable - your local IP at home, or QuickConnect, a custom domain or a VPN when you are out. Photos, camera live view, and File Station and Drive downloads all work over the QuickConnect relay, so no port forwarding is required. See remote access.
Does my data go through your servers?
Your NAS data does not. Syno Manager connects to your NAS directly and there is no MiyaraHub server in that path, so your files, readings and configuration never reach us. Credentials are held in the device keychain or keystore and are excluded from device backups, and the assistant runs its model on your phone. The one thing that does leave the device is a crash report, through Firebase Crashlytics - a stack trace, the device model and the app version, and none of your NAS data. The full detail is here.
How much does Syno Manager cost?
A one-time purchase of $6.99. No ads, no subscriptions and no in-app purchases - every feature is included, updates included.

It is priced per platform, so Google Play and the Microsoft Store are separate purchases. Apple is a single universal purchase covering iPhone, iPad and Mac - buy it once and it is on all three.

Does it support two-factor authentication?
Yes. If your DSM account uses 2FA the app prompts for your one-time code and can remember the device so you are not asked again on that phone. It also captures and sends DSM's SynoToken on every control action, which is what DSM requires of any client that changes something.
Can it manage more than one NAS?
Yes. Add as many as you like and switch between them from the device list. Each one keeps its own credentials, its own dashboard layout and its own settings. See multiple NAS devices.
