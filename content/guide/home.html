The header

A badge that says why

The top card names your model, your nickname for it and its DSM version, with a health badge on the right. The badge is not decorative - tap it and it lists what fed the verdict.

What it weighs up

  • Drive health - SMART status on every drive.
  • Volume usage - amber past 80% full, red past 90%.
  • System temperature - amber past 50°C, red past 60°C.
  • Drive temperature - amber past 45°C, red past 55°C.
  • UPS charge, when a UPS is attached.
  • Fan status and load average relative to the core count.

What it will not do

It will not show a healthy tick over a reading it could not take. If the app cannot reach the NAS, or a value came back missing, the header says that - loading, or unreachable - rather than quietly treating an absent number as a good one.

It folds too. The header is the tallest thing on the screen and it collapses like everything else. Folded, it still reports the state it is actually in: the health it read, that it is still loading, or that it cannot reach the NAS.

The quick-stats row

Five readings that answer "is it fine?" without scrolling.

ReadingWhat it tells you
TempSystem temperature, in °C or °F depending on your preference. Colours shift amber then red as it climbs.
CoolingThe fan speed mode the NAS is actually in - Full Speed, Cool or Quiet. Change it from System.
SessionsHow many connections DSM currently holds. Tap through to Active Connections to see who they are.

TimeThe NAS's own clock and the time source it syncs against. A NAS whose clock has drifted breaks two-factor codes and makes log timestamps lie.

A card that goes nowhere tells you why. If you hide a sub-view and then tap the card that opens it, the app says it is hidden and offers to take you to Customize - rather than silently landing you on some other screen, which is what a broken link feels like.
Fold what you do not read

Collapsing, and what survives it

Tap the chevron on any card to fold it. The choice is remembered per view and survives closing the app, so the dashboard opens the way you left it rather than resetting every launch.

  • A collapsed card still reports. The row keeps a status glyph, and each glyph has its own shape - a tick for healthy, a triangle for warning, a cross for a problem, a dash for unknown, an hourglass while it loads. It reads the same to anyone who cannot separate red from green, and every glyph names itself to a screen reader.
  • Long titles scroll rather than being cut. A title too wide for the collapsed row moves rather than being clipped mid-word.
  • The header folds too, and keeps reporting its real state when it does.
Edit Dashboard

Reorder, remove, reset

Tap the pencil at the top left of the home screen. The toolbar appears and every card grows a drag handle and a red minus.

  1. Drag a card by its handle to move it.
  2. Tap the red minus to take a card off the dashboard. It is not deleted, only hidden.
  3. Add opens the list of cards you have removed, so you can put one back.
  4. Reset restores the default set and order.
  5. Done leaves edit mode. The layout is saved as you go.
Cards keep working while you edit. They render live data in edit mode rather than turning into grey boxes, so you can see what you are moving. A card that is still fetching when you enter edit mode may look empty for a moment - that is the fetch, not the card.
Per NAS, not per app. Each NAS you add keeps its own layout, so a media server and a backup target do not have to look the same.
Keeping current

How often it refreshes

Live readings poll on a timer you set in Settings › Polling Interval: system values on the short interval, storage on a longer one, because volume usage does not change every five seconds and asking costs battery.

  • Pull down to refresh everything at once, whatever the interval is set to.
  • Logs do not poll. The Log Center card reads once and has a refresh button, rather than re-reading on a timer whether or not you are looking at it.
  • Polling stops in the background. The app is not draining your battery while it is not on screen.
Common questions

Home screen FAQ

What does the health badge actually mean?
It is a summary of everything the app can read: drive SMART status, volume usage, system temperature, UPS charge, fan status and load average. Tap it and it tells you which of those is responsible rather than just saying Warning. A badge that cannot read something says so instead of showing a healthy tick over a failed read.
Can I choose which cards appear?
Yes - tap the pencil to enter Edit Dashboard, then drag to reorder, tap the red minus to remove, Add to bring one back, Reset for defaults, Done to finish. Cards for services your NAS does not run are never offered.
Do collapsed cards stay collapsed?
Yes, per view, and it survives closing the app. A collapsed row still carries a status glyph with its own shape, so it reads correctly without relying on colour.
Why is a card I want missing?
Either it is switched off in Edit Dashboard, or your NAS does not run the service behind it. Every card the NAS supports is on by default, so a missing one you never touched means the package is not installed.
Can the home screen differ per NAS?
Yes. Layout, order and collapse state are stored per NAS, so each device opens the way you arranged it.
